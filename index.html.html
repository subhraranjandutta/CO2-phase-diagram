Source and method

All boundaries come from R. Span and W. Wagner, “A New Equation of State for Carbon Dioxide Covering the Fluid Region from the Triple-Point Temperature to 1100 K at Pressures up to 800 MPa”, Journal of Physical and Chemical Reference Data 25(6), 1509–1597 (1996), doi:10.1063/1.555991. This is the reference formulation used by NIST REFPROP and CoolProp.

QuantityValue or equation
Triple point216.592 K, 0.51795 MPa
Critical point304.1282 K, 7.3773 MPa
Melting curve (Eq. 3.10)p/pₜ = 1 + 1955.5390·x + 2055.4593·x², with x = T/Tₜ − 1
Sublimation curve (Eq. 3.12)ln(p/pₜ) = (Tₜ/T)·Σ aᵢ(1 − T/Tₜ)^tᵢ, a = −14.740846, 2.4327015, −5.3061778; t = 1, 1.9, 2.9
Vapour-pressure curve (Eq. 3.13)ln(p/p_c) = (T_c/T)·Σ aᵢ(1 − T/T_c)^tᵢ, a = −7.0602087, 1.9391218, −1.6463597, −3.2995634; t = 1, 1.5, 2, 4

Below the triple-point temperature, the state is solid above the sublimation curve and gas below it. Above it, the state is solid above the melting curve. Otherwise it is liquid above the vapour-pressure curve and gas below it, up to the critical temperature. At or above the critical temperature it is a supercritical fluid when the pressure is at least 7.3773 MPa, and gas below that. A state within 0.1% (in pressure) of a curve is reported as coexistence of the two phases.

Limits

  • The page treats the system as pure CO₂. Other components such as brine, N₂ or impurities shift the boundaries and the critical point, so read the result as an approximation for mixtures.
  • The melting-curve data cover 217–270 K and pressures up to about 284 MPa. Above that the curve is extrapolated.
  • The equation of state is valid to 1100 K and 800 MPa. Beyond that, further high-pressure solid forms of CO₂ exist and are not distinguished here.
  • The supercritical region is continuous. The lines at T = Tc and P = pc are conventions, not phase transitions.
